## Key Literature Reviewed

### 1. Stefanello et al. (2011) — Canine Cutaneous Perivascular Wall Tumors
- **Source:** J Vet Intern Med 2011;25:1398-1405
- **Key findings:**
  - 55 c-PWT in 53 dogs, retrospective
  - Relapse in 12 dogs (10 local, 1 metastatic, 1 both)
  - Probability of local recurrence: 2% at 6mo, 8% at 1yr, 20% at 2yr, 24% at 3yr
  - **Tumor size is the single strongest prognostic factor** — dogs with tumors >5cm had 7x higher relapse hazard
  - Margin status had clinically relevant but not statistically significant HR
  - Younger dogs tended to have smaller, lower grade tumors on extremities
  - c-PWT display favorable prognosis overall; only 2/55 developed metastases
  - Late recurrences possible (up to 1,395 days)

### 2. Burton et al. (2011) — Low-Dose Cyclophosphamide and Regulatory T Cells
- **Source:** J Vet Intern Med 2011;25:920-926
- **Key findings:**
  - 11 dogs with grade I or II STS, prospective trial
  - Dogs with STS have significantly elevated regulatory T cells (Treg) vs healthy dogs
  - CYC at 12.5 mg/m²/day: decreased absolute Treg numbers but not percentage
  - CYC at 15 mg/m²/day: decreased both Treg numbers AND percentage, AND reduced tumor microvessel density (MVD)
  - Effects selective for Treg — no changes in CD4+ or CD8+ populations
  - **Recommended dose: 15 mg/m²/day for future studies**
  - No adverse events reported over 28-day period
  - No tumor size changes by RECIST during 28-day study (expected — mechanism is antiangiogenic, not directly cytotoxic)

### 3. Elmslie, Glawe & Dow (2008) — Metronomic Cyclophosphamide + Piroxicam for STS
- **Source:** J Vet Intern Med 2008;22:1373-1379
- **Key findings:**
  - 85 dogs with incompletely resected STS (30 treated, 55 controls)
  - Treatment: CYC 10 mg/m²/day + piroxicam 0.3 mg/kg daily (both oral)
  - **DFI significantly prolonged (P < .0001)** for all tumor sites
  - Control median DFI: 211 days. Treated: median NOT REACHED (minimum projected 410+ days)
  - Significant for both trunk AND extremity tumors
  - Majority were Grade II STS (68-77%); most were hemangiopericytoma or PNST
  - **Toxicity:** 40% had mild side effects (grade 1-2). Most common: anorexia/vomiting
  - **Serious risk:** Sterile hemorrhagic cystitis — 3/30 dogs (2 grade 2, 1 grade 4 requiring euthanasia)
  - Every-other-day dosing tolerated better than daily
  - Lasix (furosemide) co-prescribed to reduce cystitis risk

### 4. Forrest et al. (2000) — Postoperative Radiotherapy for Canine STS
- **Source:** J Vet Intern Med 2000;14:578-582
- **Key findings:**
  - 35 dogs with 37 incompletely excised STS tumors + adjuvant RT
  - Total dose: 42-57 Gy in 3-4.2 Gy daily fractions, Mon-Fri schedule
  - **Overall median survival: 1,851 days (5.1 years)**
  - Median time to local recurrence: >798 days (not reached)
  - Alive at 1yr: 80%, 2yr: 72%, 3yr: 68%
  - Local recurrence-free at 1yr: 71%, 2yr: 60%, 3yr: 57%
  - **Hemangiopericytomas did best:** 92% survival, 85% local control at 3 years
  - Oral STS had significantly worse survival (1.5yr vs 6.2yr for other sites, P=.0167)
  - 14/35 dogs (40%) still alive at study end (760-1,902 days)
  - Radiation dose did not correlate with outcomes in this sample (most got 57 Gy)
  - 5 dogs (14.3%) developed pulmonary metastases

### 5. Ojeda et al. (2026) — Electrochemotherapy for Mast Cell Tumors
- **Source:** J Vet Intern Med 2026;40(1)
- **Key findings:**
  - 29 dogs, 39 MCTs treated with ECT (bleomycin + electroporation)
  - Low-grade MCTs: 100% complete remission, median survival 1,198 days
  - High-grade MCTs: 45% CR, median survival 210 days
  - ECT effective for low-grade MCTs as alternative to surgery
  - Tumor size matters — smaller tumors respond better
  - Relevant as context for MCT treatment landscape, not directly applicable to Speck's STS

### 6. Séguin et al. (2006) — Incomplete Excision of Grade II MCTs
- **Source:** J Vet Intern Med 2006;20:933-940
- **Key findings:**
  - 28 dogs, 30 incompletely excised grade II MCTs, no adjuvant therapy (except some prednisone)
  - Only 23.3% (7/30) recurred locally
  - Estimated recurrence at 1yr: 17.3%, 2yr: 22.1%, 5yr: 33.3%
  - Median overall survival: 1,426 days
  - **Local recurrence was a negative prognostic factor for overall survival (P=.04)**
  - 39.3% developed MCTs at OTHER cutaneous locations (important for monitoring)
  - Ki-67 + PCNA combination was prognostic for local recurrence (P=.03)
  - Suggests not all incompletely excised MCTs need aggressive adjuvant treatment
  - BUT recurrence when it happens negatively impacts survival

### Key Reference (cited in pathology report):
- Dennis M, et al. (2011) — Prognostic factors for cutaneous and subcutaneous STS in dogs. Vet Pathol 48(1):73-84
  - Tumors with close but clean margin <3mm: 22.5% local recurrence rate over 3 years
  - Grade 1 metastatic rate: 11%
  - Mitotic count <10: median survival 1,444 days

## Clinical Decision Framework

### STS Treatment Hierarchy (for Speck's specific case):
1. **Staging first** — chest X-rays + abdominal US (rules out metastatic disease)
2. **Radiation therapy** — preferred adjuvant given budget, access, and evidence base
3. **Consider metronomic chemo adjunct** — CYC + piroxicam post-radiation as belt-and-suspenders
4. **Re-excision** — only if surgeon believes adequate margins achievable on small dog foreleg
5. **Metronomic chemo alone** — if radiation not pursued
6. **Monitor only** — least preferred given dirty margins in a young dog

### MCT Monitoring Protocol:
- Full-body skin checks every 2-3 months
- Any new lump → FNA immediately
- Speck is an MCT dog — lifelong vigilance needed

## Important Clinical Notes
- Speck is SMALL — drug dosing, surgical margins, and radiation fields all need to account for his size
- Antebrachium (foreleg) in a chihuahua mix has very limited soft tissue — affects re-excision feasibility
- The palpable lump at STS site needs characterization before radiation planning
- Late recurrences are documented up to 1,395 days — monitoring must continue for YEARS
- Cystitis monitoring is essential if metronomic CYC is used — periodic urinalysis
