if the compression test is okay, what might it be?

If compression comes back good across all four cylinders, the misfire is almost certainly from something external to the hard parts: air, fuel, spark control, timing, or wiring/ECU.

1. Air / Vacuum / Boost leak
Unmetered air (post-MAF leak, cracked intercooler hose, loose clamp, bad intake gasket) will cause lean misfires, especially at idle or under certain loads.
How to check: Smoke test the intake and intercooler system. Spray brake cleaner/carb cleaner around suspect joints at idle and listen for RPM change.

2. MAF / Sensor / Timing issue
Dirty or failing MAF sensor skews load calculation → wrong fueling and timing → misfire with otherwise "good" compression.
Cam or crank position sensor issues, or slightly off timing belt (one tooth) can cause misfire with normal compression numbers.
How to check: Clean MAF with proper MAF cleaner, log or watch live data for weird readings. Verify timing marks on the belt line up perfectly on both cams and crank. Scan for cam/crank correlation or other sensor codes.

3. Fuel delivery / fuel pressure
You've ruled out injectors themselves, but fuel pressure, regulator, pump or wiring to a given injector can still cause a lean or dead cylinder.
How to check: Measure fuel pressure at the rail under idle and load. Use a noid light or scope to confirm injector pulse on the misfiring cylinder(s).

4. Ignition wiring / grounds / connector issues
New coils and plugs don't help if a connector isn't fully seated, a pin is bent, harness is chafed, or a ground is corroded.
How to check: Wiggle-test the harness with the engine idling and watch for changes. Inspect coil and injector connectors individually; check and clean engine grounds.

5. ECU / control issue (rare but real)
If coils, injectors, compression, wiring, and sensors all check out, a failing driver in the ECU can cause a persistent misfire on one cylinder. This is a last-resort suspect once everything else has been verified.

Practically, what to do next if compression is OK:
1. Smoke test intake/IC system for leaks.
2. Clean and log MAF; verify front O2 is behaving normally.
3. Confirm timing marks on belt and check cam/crank sensor signals.
4. Check injector pulse and fuel pressure under load.
5. Inspect harness/grounds around the affected cylinders and coils/injectors.

what does the pcv valve do?

PCV valve = "crankcase vacuum cleaner" for your EJ205.

ELI5 What It Does:
When pistons fire, some combustion gas sneaks past the rings into the crankcase (called "blow-by"). Without a PCV, this builds pressure, contaminates oil with acids/moisture, creates sludge, and can blow seals.

The PCV valve:
1. Draws crankcase vapors OUT via engine vacuum.
2. Routes them back INTO the intake to be burned.
3. Regulates flow based on engine load.

Result: No pressure buildup, clean oil, no sludge, no blown seals.

When it fails (stuck closed): Pressure builds → seals blow → oil leaks everywhere.
When it fails (stuck open): Too much oil vapor → fouled intake, rough idle, oil in intercooler.

On turbocharged EJ205s: Critical because boost pressure + blow-by = accelerated sludge. Oil catch can intercepts the vapor before it gums up your intake valves.
