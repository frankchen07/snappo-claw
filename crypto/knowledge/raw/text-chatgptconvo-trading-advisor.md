## 🪙 What is Alt Season?

**Alt season** (short for *altcoin season*) is a period when **altcoins (everything that’s not BTC)** significantly outperform Bitcoin. You’ll typically see:

- **High % gains across dozens of altcoins**
- **Explosive moves in low/mid-cap coins**
- **BTC either consolidating or slowly moving up**
- **Retail sentiment returning (e.g., trending coins, NFTs, Discords popping off)**

In essence: **Risk-on for crypto**.

---

## 📊 What is Bitcoin Dominance?

**Bitcoin Dominance (BTC.D)** measures the percentage of the total crypto market cap that’s made up by Bitcoin. Formulaically:

> **BTC Dominance = BTC Market Cap / Total Crypto Market Cap**

It’s one of the most useful macro indicators in crypto.

### When BTC.D goes **up**:
- Bitcoin is gaining market share (e.g., altcoins are underperforming or bleeding).
- This typically happens when:
  - BTC rallies strongly (altcoins can’t keep up),
  - or when the market is fearful and capital flees to BTC as a safe haven.

### When BTC.D goes **down**:
- Altcoins are outperforming.
- This is your **alt season trigger**.
- It suggests capital is rotating into higher-risk coins.

---

## 🔍 How to Tell When Alt Season is Starting

### 1. **Watch BTC.D** (Use [TradingView: BTC.D](https://www.tradingview.com/symbols/CRYPTOCAP-BTC.D/))

- **Breakdown of BTC.D** from major support = alt season ignition.

  - **Double tops** (signaling BTC.D exhaustion)
  - **Clean trendline breaks**
  - Lower highs on BTC.D while alts are gaining momentum

### 2. **BTC Sideways = Good for Alts**
- When BTC chops in a tight range, alts often fly.

## **1. RSI (Relative Strength Index)**  
- **Type:** *Momentum oscillator*  
- **Range:** 0 to 100  
- **Purpose:** Shows whether a stock/coin is *overbought* or *oversold* **based on its own recent price history**.  
- **Calculation:** Compares the size of recent gains vs. recent losses over a set period (usually 14 days).

**Key points:**
- RSI > 70 → Often called “overbought”  
- RSI < 30 → Often called “oversold”  
- **O’Neil/Minervini don’t rely on this** — because strong trends can stay > 70 for weeks.

---

## **2. RS Line (Relative Strength Line in O’Neil’s system)**  
- **Type:** *Performance comparison line*  
- **Purpose:** Shows how the asset is performing vs. a benchmark index (e.g., BTC for crypto, S&P 500 for stocks).  
- **Calculation:**  
  \[
  \text{RS Line} = \frac{\text{Price of Asset}}{\text{Price of Benchmark}}
  \]
- If the RS line is going **up** → your asset is outperforming the benchmark.  
- If the RS line is going **down** → it’s underperforming.

**Key points:**
- O’Neil’s *Relative Strength Rating* (0–99) is based on this over 12 months.
- Minervini loves **strong RS lines hitting new highs before price breaks out** — it’s a leadership signal.

| Feature       | RSI (Relative Strength Index)        | RS Line (Relative Strength vs Index) |
|---------------|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Measures      | Momentum vs. its own past            | Performance vs. benchmark            |
| Scale         | 0–100                                | No fixed scale                        |

Both **O’Neil** and **Minervini** basically ignore the order book in their trading — they’re looking at **price + volume over time**, not microsecond-level liquidity.  

But in **crypto**, especially for intraday entries, the order book can give you *extra context* if you know how to use it.  

---

## **How the Order Book Works**
- **Bids** = buy orders (people waiting to buy)
- **Asks** = sell orders (people waiting to sell)
- **Order book depth** shows *how many* coins are waiting at each price level.

---

## **3 Main Uses for Swing Trading**
(not scalping — just to supplement O’Neil/Minervini-style setups)

### **1. Spotting Liquidity Walls**
- A huge cluster of buy orders (“bid wall”) below price = potential short-term support.
- A huge cluster of sell orders (“ask wall”) above price = potential short-term resistance.
- Useful to know if your breakout has to “eat through” a lot of resting sell orders.

---

### **2. Detecting Absorption**
- If a big sell wall keeps getting *eaten away* without price dropping → shows strong buying demand (bullish).
- If a big buy wall keeps getting *chewed up* without price bouncing → shows selling pressure (bearish).

---

### **3. Avoiding Illiquid Whipsaws**
- In small-cap coins (like GTC), thin order books = easy for whales to move price.
- If your trade idea depends on tight stops, a thin order book can get you wicked out even in a good setup.

---

## **The Big Caveats**
- **Spoofing**: Big orders can appear/disappear to fake sentiment — especially in crypto.
- **No lasting predictive power**: Order book can change in seconds; O’Neil/Minervini focus on patterns that play out over days/weeks.
- **Don’t replace the chart**: Always confirm with price + volume patterns.

---

💡 **How I use it personally in crypto swing trades**:
- Main analysis = daily & 4H chart with O’Neil/Minervini rules  
- Order book = quick glance before entry to see if there’s a wall right above my breakout level or if liquidity is too thin to size up safely

---

## **1. Setup**
- A **setup** is the *chart pattern or condition* that tells you *when and where to act*.  
- Examples:  
  - Cup with Handle  
  - Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP)  
  - Double Bottom  
  - Breakout above resistance with volume  

👉 A setup gives you a *specific trade idea* with clear entry, stop, and (ideally) target.  

---

## **2. Edge**
- An **edge** is the *reason that setup works over the long run*.  
- It’s not a single pattern — it’s your **statistical advantage** in the market.  
- Comes from:  
  - Historical study (e.g., O’Neil’s CANSLIM showed leaders repeatedly form similar bases before massive moves).  
  - Psychology (breakouts work because shorts cover + new buyers pile in).  
  - Risk management (keeping losers small and letting winners run).  

👉 An edge is your *repeatable advantage*. A setup is just *one way to apply it*.  

---

## **Think of it Like This**
- **Setup = trigger**  
- **Edge = reason you make money if you repeat that trigger 100 times**  

Example with crypto:  
- **Setup:** ETH forms a cup with handle, breaks out above $3,800 on volume.  
- **Edge:** You know from O’Neil’s research that liquid leaders breaking out of bases, with strong RS and earnings (or in crypto, strong narratives), have a higher chance of running 20–50% before topping.  

---

⚡️ **Minervini would say:**  
- A trader without an edge is just gambling setups.  
- A trader with an edge but no setups can’t execute consistently.  
- You need **both** — the edge tells you *why*, the setup tells you *when*.

---

## 🧩 **1. Definitions**
- **Closing Range (CR):**  
  Measures *where* the candle closes within its high–low range.  
  \[
  \text{CR} = \frac{(\text{Close} - \text{Low})}{(\text{High} - \text{Low})}
  \]  
  → 100 % = closed at the high (buyers dominate).  
  → 0 % = closed at the low (sellers dominate).  
  → 50 % = neutral.  

- **Average True Range (ATR):**  
  Measures *how large* that candle’s range is compared to recent volatility.  
  It’s an absolute or normalized measure of volatility.  

---

## 🧠 **2. Why They Work Best Together**

- **Closing range** tells *who won* the battle.  
- **ATR** tells *how big* the battle was.  
- Together, they reveal *conviction strength*: did that victory happen on a mild skirmish or a full-blown war?

---

## ⚔️ **3. Interpreting the Combinations**

| Closing Range | ATR Context | Meaning |
|----------------|--------------|---------|
| **High CR (70–100%) + Small ATR** | Quiet, low-vol move closing near highs | Controlled accumulation; stealth buying. |
| **High CR (70–100%) + Large ATR** | Huge candle closing near highs | Strong bullish conviction; likely breakout or institutional push. |
| **Low CR (0–30%) + Small ATR** | Weak close but low volatility | Drifting down, mild distribution. |
| **Low CR (0–30%) + Large ATR** | Big selloff closing near lows | Capitulation; panic or strong distribution. |
| **Mid CR (40–60%) + Large ATR** | Huge range, mid close | Tug-of-war; buyers and sellers both active, indecision zone. |
| **Mid CR (40–60%) + Shrinking ATR over time** | Tight range, balanced closes | Volatility contraction → base forming (VCP behavior). |

---

## 🧮 **4. How to Quantify This**

You can normalize both metrics:
- Express **CR** as a percentage (0–100%).  
- Express **ATR** relative to average ATR (e.g., today’s ATR ÷ 20-day ATR).  
Then you can scan for:
- **High CR + High ATR (>1.2× avg)** → breakout strength.  
- **Low CR + High ATR** → distribution or panic.  
- **High CR + Shrinking ATR** → quiet accumulation setup.  

This is basically what pros like **Mark Minervini** are doing intuitively when they say “tight price action after volatility expansion.”
